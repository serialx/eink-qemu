eink-qemu

An emulator for ESP32 e-ink readers: the Xteink X4 Pro, X3 and X4, and the BOOX Picco. It runs their unmodified firmware, stock or any fork, with the screen, SD card, keys and Wi-Fi behaving like the real board. This app runs native QEMU.Everything runs here in your browser.

Built on QEMU. Started as a fork of prokrypt/x4prosim. Runs in the browser on Kohei Tokunaga's qemu-wasm JIT.

Wi-Fi

Gives the emulated reader an open Wi-Fi network, PICSimLabWifi, on QEMU's user network, so the firmware reaches the internet.whose HTTP and HTTPS downloads your browser fetches. Join it from the firmware's own Wi-Fi settings.

Sites must allow cross-origin downloads, and HTTPS needs firmware that accepts the emulator's local certificate (firmware that checks certificates rejects it). Files this page bundles, such as CrossPoint Reader's font catalog when it was packaged, are served from the page itself: nothing goes through a relay server.

Off, the reader finds no networks. Applies when the emulator starts. ?wifi=off opens the page with it off.

E-ink ghosting

Available on X3 and X4 Pro. X4 and Picco show completed frames without ghosting.

Simulates faint traces of previous images and gradual fading, like a real e-ink panel. Turn it off for clean, stable pixels when comparing screenshots.

Applies when the emulator starts. ?ghosting=0 opens the page with it off.

Turbo: fast, not timing-accurate

Runs the device as fast as your computer allows: SD card, screen refresh and SPI transfer delays are cut to a minimum. Booting and page turns take a fraction of the time. Idle time still passes in real time, so a resting device keeps its screen and your CPU stays free.Idle waits are skipped too.

Timing no longer matches real hardware, so log timestamps and refresh times are not realistic. Turn it off to see how fast the firmware runs on an actual device.

Applies when the emulator starts. ?turbo=0 opens the page with it off.

SD card

Drop files or folders here to copy them into the open folder. Changing the card while the emulator runs resets the device so it mounts the card again.

Choose firmware to begin

Pick an ESP32-C3 or ESP32-S3 firmware: an app .bin (it goes into the Arduino-ESP32 16 MB partition table), a merged .bin, a 16 MiB flash dump, or an official X4 Pro .xota update. For example, crosspoint-<ver>-x3-x4.bin or -x4pro.bin from the CrossPoint Reader releases. An S3 image runs on the X4 Pro unless you pick the Picco; for a C3 image pick the X3 or the X4 in the firmware card. Drop the file here or choose it; loading it starts the emulator.

The SD card is an image file on disk. A blank 16 GiB FAT32 card is created on first run; it takes a few MiB until files are written.

Your SD card is saved in this browser. On your first visit a blank 64 MiB FAT32 card is used.
